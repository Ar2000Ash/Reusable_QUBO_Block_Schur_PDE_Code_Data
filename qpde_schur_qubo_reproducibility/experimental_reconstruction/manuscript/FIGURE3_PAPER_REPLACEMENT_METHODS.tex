All five PDE families in Table~\ref{tab:benchmark_protocol} are then tested at
\begin{equation}
    B=2,\qquad M=1,\qquad K=10,
    \label{eq:fixed_validation_encoding}
\end{equation}
corresponding to 24 logical bits per inverse-column QUBO. The initial scales
are $\gamma_0=0.60$ for Heat and Burgers, $\gamma_0=1.00$ for
Klein--Gordon, and $\gamma_0=0.01$ for Poisson and Helmholtz. One initial
QUBO solve and three QUBO residual corrections are applied to every inverse
column. Starting from $\by_{ij}^{(0)}=\bm{0}$, the prescribed schedule is
\begin{align}
  \gamma_p &= \gamma_0 8^{-p},\quad p=0,1,2,3,
    \label{eq:five_pde_scale_schedule}\\
  \bm r_{ij}^{(p)} &= \be_j-\bS_i\by_{ij}^{(p)},\notag\\
  \bq_{ij,p}^{*} &\in \underset{\bq\in\{0,1\}^{24}}{\operatorname{arg\,min}}
    \|\bS_i\bP(\gamma_p)\bq-\bm r_{ij}^{(p)}\|_2^2,\notag\\
  \by_{ij}^{(p+1)} &= \by_{ij}^{(p)}+\bP(\gamma_p)\bq_{ij,p}^{*}.
    \label{eq:five_pde_multiscale}
\end{align}
The factor eight is fixed before solving any QUBO. No continuous correction
or classical inverse is used to choose a scale or a binary answer; a
continuous correction is calculated only retrospectively to audit its
representability. The discretizations, manufactured forcing, initial
conditions, Dirichlet traces, time steps and inverse-column call budgets
remain unchanged. Successor Schur blocks use the preceding decoded quantized
inverse factors.

The new finite-bit minima are computed with an exact two-scalar reduced-grid
CPU oracle. This solves the same 24-bit least-squares QUBO minimization
without exhaustive traversal of all $2^{24}$ binary assignments, and is not
a new GPU or QCI hardware timing experiment. A separately rerun unchanged-grid
control uses $\gamma_p=\gamma_0$ for all four stages; its residual corrections
all return zero.
