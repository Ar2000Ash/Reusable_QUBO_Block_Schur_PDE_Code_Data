\paragraph{Predetermined multiscale five-PDE validation.}
All five PDE benchmarks use $B=2$, $M=1$ and $K=10$, with a 24-variable
least-squares QUBO per Schur inverse column. The initial scale $\gamma_0$
is $0.60$ for Heat/Burgers, $0.01$ for Poisson/Helmholtz and $1.00$
for Klein--Gordon. One initial solve and three residual corrections use
\begin{equation}
  r_p=e_j-S_i y_p,\qquad
  z_p^*\in\arg\min_{z\in\{0,1\}^{24}}\|S_i P(\gamma_p)z-r_p\|_2^2,\qquad
  y_{p+1}=y_p+P(\gamma_p)z_p^*,\qquad
  \gamma_p=\gamma_0 8^{-p},\ p=0,1,2,3.
  \label{eq:five_pde_multiscale}
\end{equation}
The factor eight is specified before solving any QUBO and is shared by all five
problems. It does not use a classical inverse or an adaptive correction-range
oracle; a continuous correction is evaluated only retrospectively to audit
representability. Each successor Schur block uses the previously decoded
quantized inverse. The spatial operators, source terms, time increments,
manufactured initial conditions and analytical Dirichlet traces remain
unchanged. The new fixed-$\gamma$ control is retained separately as an
ablation: on that single unchanged lattice all three corrections were zero.
An exact reduced-grid \emph{CPU} oracle certifies every two-scalar finite-grid
minimum by conditional quadratic minimization. This is mathematically
an exact 24-bit QUBO ground-state result, but it is not a new full-space
GPU enumeration or hardware runtime measurement. This newly specified
multiscale study is not a recovery of the historical unarchived
\texttt{auto\_bound} implementation.

The new multiscale procedure yields relative errors
$1.34\times10^{-5}$ (Heat), $1.10\times10^{-5}$ (Burgers),
$3.05\times10^{-6}$ (Poisson), $6.88\times10^{-6}$ (Helmholtz) and
$2.30\times10^{-5}$ (Klein--Gordon), compared with the separate dense
numerical references. The displacement and velocity component errors in
the Klein--Gordon case are $1.42\times10^{-5}$ and $1.28\times10^{-4}$,
respectively, and should be reported separately from the combined-state
error. The five tests use the same local 24-bit encoding and initial scales;
local accuracy improves because the three residual corrections are encoded
on successively finer, predetermined grids. Figure~\ref{fig:fixed_validation}
separates the multiscale result from the earlier fixed-grid control. The unchanged-$\gamma$ control stagnates, so repeated exact optimization
on the same lattice cannot be credited with the improvement. The present
results are numerical evidence for this particular schedule and these
five systems, not a general theorem of multiscale convergence or a
hardware coefficient-precision result. Exact full-space CUDA timings and
QCI experiments are not inferred from the CPU reduced-grid run.
